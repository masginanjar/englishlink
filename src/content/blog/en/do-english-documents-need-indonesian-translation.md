Many people assume English is understood everywhere, so an English document should be fine as it is. In daily life that is often true. For official matters in Indonesia, it frequently is not.

## Who usually asks for an Indonesian version?

| Situation | Typical documents |
|---|---|
| Registering an overseas marriage or birth | Marriage certificate, birth certificate issued abroad |
| Having a foreign degree recognised | Diploma and transcript |
| Court or notary matters | Contracts, powers of attorney, statements |
| Setting up or running a company | Deeds, resolutions, agreements with foreign parties |
| Residence and work permits | Supporting letters and certificates |

The exact list is always set by the office handling your case, so treat this table as orientation, not a rulebook.

## Three questions to settle it

1. **Will the document be filed with an Indonesian authority?** If yes, ask them which language they accept.
2. **Does their checklist mention a sworn translator?** Then a regular translation will not do.
3. **Is the document only for you or your team to read?** Then you may not need a translation, or a regular one is enough.

## Things that surprise foreign residents

- **Names stay as written.** Your name is not adapted to Indonesian spelling.
- **Stamps and handwritten notes are translated too.** Anything on the page that carries meaning is part of the document.
- **Layout follows the original.** That makes it easy for an officer to compare the two versions line by line.
- **An apostille on the foreign document may be requested separately.** Translation and authentication are different steps.

## A common case

A couple married abroad returns to Indonesia and wants the marriage recorded locally. They bring the foreign certificate, in English, and are asked for an Indonesian translation by a sworn translator. They had assumed the English original was sufficient. A quick call to the office beforehand would have saved a second trip.

## How to prepare

1. Get the official checklist from the office concerned.
2. Photograph the document front and back, including any apostille or legalization page.
3. Tell the translator which office will receive it.
4. Check names, dates and numbers in the result against the original.

## FAQ

### Can I submit a bilingual contract instead?
If the contract was drafted in both languages, the office may accept it. Ask them, because practice differs.

### Do I need to send the original paper document?
For review and a quote, a clear photo or scan is enough. Whether the original must be shown later depends on the receiving office.

### Is the translation valid for other offices too?
Often yes, but each office can have its own requirements. Confirm before reusing it.
