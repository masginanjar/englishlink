University and scholarship applications are a relay race: several batons must arrive at the same time. Documents are often the baton that arrives last. To avoid that, split the preparation into three rounds.

## Round 1: collect

Well before applications open, put everything in one folder.

- Diploma and transcript, including legalized pages.
- A passport with plenty of validity left.
- Birth certificate and family card.
- Language test certificate.
- Relevant achievement or organisation certificates.

## Round 2: map

Once the official requirements are out, make a simple table.

| Document | Language required | Authentication? | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diploma | English | Check university rules | Pending |
| Transcript | English | Check university rules | Pending |
| Birth certificate | English | Check visa rules | Pending |
| Sponsor's bank statement | English | Not mentioned | Pending |

The status column is there on purpose, so you can see progress at a glance.

## Round 3: work in parallel

Do not wait for one task to finish before starting the next. While you write your essay, your documents can be translated. While you wait for a recommendation letter, you can check the translation.

## Three traps

1. **Inconsistent names.** Diploma, passport and language certificate must point to the same person. If they differ, prepare an explanation.
2. **Forgotten financial documents.** They are often a student visa requirement.
3. **Assuming university and visa requirements are the same.** Two institutions, two lists.

## The suitcase comparison

Preparing documents is like packing for a long trip. People who pack the night before nearly always forget something. People who write a list a week earlier leave calmly. Your list here is the table in round two.

## Keep everything in one place

Create a cloud folder with subfolders by type: academic, identity, financial and supporting. Use consistent file names such as `diploma-original` and `diploma-translation`. When the portal opens at midnight and you must upload ten files, that tidy folder will feel like a lifesaver.

## About scholarships

Each provider has its own document list, format and deadline, and these can change from year to year. Rely on their official page, not on screenshots passed around.

## FAQ

### When is the best time to translate a diploma?
As soon as you are sure you will apply and have the checklist. A diploma does not change, so there is no reason to wait.

### Do recommendation letters need translating?
If written in Indonesian and the recipient wants English, yes. Many referees prefer to write in English directly.

### Can the same translation be used for several universities?
Often yes, as long as each university's requirements are met. Check them one by one.
