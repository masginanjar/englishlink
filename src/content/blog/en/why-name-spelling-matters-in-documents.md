Indonesian names are often written in more than one way over a lifetime. Family and colleagues know that "Soetrisno" and "Sutrisno" are one person. A university admissions office or an immigration desk in another country does not, and it reads paper one letter at a time.

## Why does a small difference cause trouble?

Think of a name as a key. A key with one tooth out of place looks almost right and still does not open the door. Application systems and reviewing officers work the same way: the name on one document is matched against the others, usually with the passport as the reference.

A mismatch rarely means an instant refusal. It means a question: explain this, attach supporting papers, or correct the record. Each of those takes time, often when a deadline is close.

## Six spelling patterns worth checking

These are common patterns, not real individuals.

| Pattern | Document A | Document B |
|---|---|---|
| Old and current spelling | Soetrisno, Djoko | Sutrisno, Joko |
| Double letters | Muhammad, Fitriyani | Muhamad, Fitriani |
| Spaces and hyphens | Nur Aini | Nuraini |
| Abbreviated names | M. Rizal | Muhammad Rizal |
| Name order | Wijaya Kusuma, Andi | Andi Wijaya Kusuma |
| Titles included | Dr. Ratna Sari, S.H. | Ratna Sari |

Two points help readers outside Indonesia. First, older documents use a pre-reform spelling in which "oe" stands for "u" and "dj" for "j". Second, many Indonesians have no family name at all, so a form that asks for a first and last name forces a choice that can end up different on each form.

## Which documents differ most often?

Differences usually turn up between these pairs:

- **Birth certificate and passport.** The certificate may be decades old, while the passport follows current ID data.
- **Diploma and passport.** Schools keep their own records.
- **Marriage book and ID card.** A spouse's or parent's name is spelled another way.
- **Bank statement and passport.** Banks shorten long names.

Parents' names matter too, since they appear on a birth certificate and may be compared with other family documents.

## Map the spellings in 15 minutes

1. Gather every document that will be submitted.
2. Make a two-column table: the document, and the name exactly as written there, spaces and abbreviations included.
3. Add rows for parents and spouse where their documents are part of the file.
4. Mark every row that differs from the passport.
5. For each marked row, ask the receiving institution whether an explanation is needed and which supporting document it accepts.
6. Keep the table and reuse it for every form.

Send the same table to your translator. It shows at a glance which spelling belongs to the source document and which to the passport.

## Can the translator match the name to the passport?

No. An official translation follows the source document. If the certificate says "Soetrisno", so does the translation, even when the passport says "Sutrisno". A sworn translator has no authority to correct a document.

| The situation | Who can help |
|---|---|
| The original document contains an error | The office that issued it |
| Two valid documents use different spellings | The receiving institution, which decides what explanation it accepts |
| The translation does not match the source | The translator, through a recheck |

## FAQ

### How should a single-word name be entered on a form with first and last name fields?
Rules differ between institutions, so follow the official instructions of the one you are applying to. Use the same approach on every form for that application.

### Does an old spelling such as "oe" or "dj" have to be changed first?
Not necessarily. The old spelling remains what the original document says. Ask the receiving institution whether it wants an explanation for the difference from the passport.

### When is the best time to check name spelling?
Before applying and before translation, while there is still time to ask the issuing office or the receiving institution.
