There is no single master list that applies to everyone. Still, the pattern is clear enough to group documents into four families.

## Four families of documents

| Family | Includes | Usually for |
|---|---|---|
| Civil records | Birth certificate, family card, ID card, marriage and divorce certificates | Visas, cross-border marriage, relocation |
| Education | Diploma, transcript, graduation letter, report cards | University, scholarships, degree recognition |
| Financial | Bank statements, payslips, sponsor letters | Student and visitor visas |
| Legal and business | Company deeds, contracts, powers of attorney, court rulings | Partnerships, tenders, court matters |

## Checklist: official or not?

Tick what applies.

- [ ] The document goes to an embassy, university, court or government office.
- [ ] The requirements use the words *sworn*, *certified* or *official translation*.
- [ ] The document proves someone's identity, status or rights.
- [ ] The recipient wants the translation attached to a copy of the original.
- [ ] The document will be legalized or apostilled after translation.

One tick is reason enough to ask the recipient. Three or more, and you almost certainly need an official translation.

## Often assumed mandatory, but not always

- **CVs and cover letters.** Usually written directly in English.
- **Short-course certificates.** It depends on whether the recipient treats them as key evidence.
- **Ordinary emails and letters.** Understanding them is enough, unless they become evidence in court.

## The document people forget

Someone preparing a student visa lines up the diploma and transcript neatly, then realises at the last minute that the sponsor's bank statement must also be in English. Financial papers are forgotten most often because they look like "just numbers". Transaction descriptions, bank names and account types are still text that needs translating.

A practical habit: when the checklist comes out, rewrite it as a three-column table with the document name, the language requested and whether authentication is needed. The order of work becomes obvious.

## Which to handle first

Start with the documents other documents rely on, namely the birth certificate and family card, because the names there set the spelling for everything that follows. Education records come next, then financial papers, which usually need to be the most recent.

## FAQ

### Does an ID card need translating?
Sometimes, as a supporting document for visas or cross-border marriages. Follow the recipient's checklist.

### What if my document is already bilingual?
Some recipients accept it as is, others still ask for an official translation. Check before ordering.

### Can only some pages be translated?
Documents are normally translated in full so nothing loses its context. If the recipient asks for specific pages only, say so at the start.
