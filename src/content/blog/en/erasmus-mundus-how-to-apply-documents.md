Every year-end, Indonesian graduates start asking the same two things about Erasmus Mundus: where does the application go, and what paperwork should be ready? The answers below come from the European Commission's official Erasmus+ page, as checked on 20 November 2026.

## What is an Erasmus Mundus Joint Master?

It is a master's programme delivered jointly by several universities. The official page says each one involves at least 3 institutions from at least 3 different countries. Programmes last 1 to 2 academic years, worth 60, 90 or 120 ECTS credits.

Graduates receive either a joint degree (one certificate issued on behalf of at least two institutions) or multiple degrees (at least two certificates from two institutions). The exact form is fixed in a study agreement at the start of your studies.

## Who can apply?

Students from all over the world are welcome. The basic conditions on the official page are:

1. You hold a bachelor's degree, or you are in the final year of one.
2. If you are still studying, you must graduate before the master's programme starts.
3. An equivalent level of learning also counts, provided it is officially recognised in the degree-awarding country.

Full scholarships may be offered to the best-ranked students. They cover participation costs and contribute to travel, visa and a living allowance. The official page gives no amounts, so look for figures on each programme's own site.

## Where and when do you apply?

This is where many applicants go wrong. There is no central EU application. You apply directly to the institution running your chosen programme.

On timing, the official page says that in most cases applications are submitted between October and January for courses starting the following academic year. That is a general window, not a deadline. Each programme sets its own closing date, and some fall earlier than people expect.

A safe order of work:

1. Browse the Erasmus Mundus catalogue and shortlist programmes that fit your background.
2. Open each programme's website. It lists the partner universities, the entry requirements and every step of the application.
3. Put the closing date in your calendar and count backwards for each document.

## Which Indonesian documents can you prepare now?

The Erasmus+ page does not list documents, because each programme decides. The table below is therefore not an official requirement. It is a worksheet to fill in while reading the programme's page.

| Document you hold | Question to check on the programme site | Your note |
|---|---|---|
| Bachelor's diploma (*ijazah*) | Which language is accepted? Is certification needed? | |
| Academic transcript | Is a grading scale explanation required? | |
| Provisional graduation letter | Is it accepted while the diploma is pending? | |
| English test certificate | Which tests and what scores? | |
| Passport | Is it required at the application stage? | |
| Reference letters | How many, and who submits them? | |

Indonesian universities generally issue diplomas and transcripts in Indonesian, sometimes with an English version. If the programme wants English documents and your university does not provide them, you will need a translation. Who may translate, and whether certification is needed, depends on the programme's own rules.

## A common scenario

An applicant opens the programme site two weeks before it closes. It asks for an English transcript, but the university only issues an Indonesian one and its office is shut for the year-end break. The essay is strong and the file is incomplete. Reading the programme page first would have let the paperwork run alongside the essay writing.

## FAQ

### Do I apply for Erasmus Mundus through the European Union?
No. The official Erasmus+ website says students apply directly to the institution running their chosen master's programme.

### Can final-year undergraduates apply?
Yes, as long as you graduate before the master's programme starts.

### Is there a single Erasmus Mundus deadline?
No. Most programmes take applications between October and January, and each one publishes its own closing date.
