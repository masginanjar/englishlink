A birth certificate (*akta kelahiran*) is a single sheet. Because it is short, people rarely check it properly. Yet it is requested for almost every visa, scholarship and cross-border marriage.

## The seven mistakes

1. **The name differs from the passport.** Older certificates sometimes use an older spelling. The translation follows the certificate, so you need to know about the difference early.
2. **Dates written in digits only.** `05/06/2000` can be read as 5 June or 6 May. The month should be written in letters.
3. **Parents' names go unchecked.** Many authorities compare them with other documents.
4. **Margin notes are missed.** A change of name or an acknowledgement is often recorded in the margin or on the back.
5. **An old-format certificate is photographed halfway.** Older certificates are large and sometimes folded. Make sure the whole sheet is visible.
6. **The certificate number is blurry.** The small digits at the top are the ones most often unreadable.
7. **Assuming one translation lasts forever.** Some recipients want a recent translation or one with specific authentication.

| What often happens | What should happen |
|---|---|

| Only the front is sent | Front and back are sent |
| The purpose is not mentioned | The country and institution are stated |

## Why older certificates are riskier

Older certificates often use the old spelling system, handwriting or faded stamps. Unreadable parts must not be guessed. You will usually be asked for a new photo or a companion document such as the family card.

## A quick quiz

The certificate says *Soesanto*. The passport says *Susanto*. Which goes in the translation?

**Answer:** what the certificate says, *Soesanto*. A translation may not alter the source. The difference from the passport is handled through whatever supporting documents the receiving authority asks for.

## Checklist before sending

- [ ] Front and back photographed, no glare.
- [ ] Certificate number and dates clearly readable.
- [ ] Your name and your parents' names compared with the passport.
- [ ] Destination country and institution mentioned.

## FAQ

### Should the certificate be legalized before translation?
That depends on the destination country and institution. Ask them for the order of steps, or tell us the destination when you get in touch.

### Part of my certificate is damaged. What happens?
The missing part is noted in the translation, never filled in by estimate. If the damage is severe, consider obtaining a new extract.

### Does the family card need translating too?
Often yes, since the two complement each other. Confirm with your checklist.
