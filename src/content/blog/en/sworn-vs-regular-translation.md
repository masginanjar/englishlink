Both move the content of a document into another language. What differs is **who does the work** and **where the result is accepted**.

## Side by side

| | Sworn translation | Regular translation |
|---|---|---|
| Done by | An appointed, sworn-in translator | Any capable translator |
| Comes with | Statement, signature and stamp | No certification |
| Used for | Visas, universities, courts, government offices | CVs, emails, content, personal reading |
| Layout | Follows the source document | Flexible |

## The 30-second test

Answer three questions:

1. **Who receives the document?** An embassy, university, court or government office usually means official.
2. **Does the checklist say "sworn", "certified" or "official"?** If it does, the answer is settled.
3. **If the translation is wrong, who bears the consequences?** If a rejected file or a disputed agreement is on the line, do not take the risk.

Two or more "yes" answers point to a sworn translation.

## A real-world pattern

A company receives a 40-page draft contract from a prospective partner overseas. The internal team only needs to understand it for a meeting, so they order a regular translation that can be read quickly. Once both sides agree and the final contract must be filed with an authority, the final version is translated officially.

That approach makes sense. A document that is still changing does not need certifying, only understanding. What gets certified is the final version that is actually used.

## Mistakes people make

- **Ordering a regular translation, then getting rejected.** Read the requirements first.
- **Assuming sworn means valid everywhere.** Some countries still ask for an apostille or legalization.
- **Translating it yourself and asking for a stamp.** A sworn translator is responsible for the content, so the document is still translated and reviewed by them.

## What happens if you choose wrong?

Ordering sworn when nobody asked for it only means paying more than necessary. Ordering regular when sworn was required can get your file returned and push your schedule back. When in doubt and the deadline is tight, ask the recipient first.

## FAQ

### Does a sworn translation cost more?
It can, because it carries the translator's responsibility and certification. The exact amount depends on the document, so ask for a quote first.

### Is a sworn translation more accurate?
Both should be accurate. The difference is the formal statement from the translator confirming it matches the source.
