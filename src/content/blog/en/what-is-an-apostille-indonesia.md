If you hold an Indonesian birth certificate, diploma or notarial deed and need to use it overseas, someone will sooner or later ask for an apostille. This guide explains what that means in Indonesia, based on the ministerial regulation and the HCCH website, as of 24 November 2026.

## What does an apostille actually certify?

Indonesia's Minister of Law and Human Rights Regulation No. 6 of 2022 defines an apostille as the act of certifying an official's signature, stamp and/or official seal on a document, based on verification.

So it confirms who signed and sealed the paper. It says nothing about whether the content is correct. Think of a bank checking a signature card: the clerk confirms the signature is real, not that the letter makes sense.

According to the HCCH, the Apostille Convention entered into force for Indonesia on 4 June 2022.

## Which Indonesian documents qualify?

The regulation lists four groups:

1. Documents from authorities connected with the courts, such as a court registrar or bailiff.
2. Administrative documents.
3. Notarial documents.
4. Official certificates attached to documents signed by private persons.

Three groups are excluded: documents signed by diplomatic or consular officials, administrative documents dealing directly with commercial or customs operations, and documents issued by the prosecution service.

## Do you need one for your case?

| Your situation | Apostille? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Destination country is a party and the institution asks for it | Yes | Apply for the documents they list |
| Destination country is a party, but nobody asked | Maybe not | Ask the institution in writing |
| Destination country is not a party | Not available | Follow that embassy's legalisation rules |
| Document will be used inside Indonesia | No | Follow the Indonesian institution's rules |

To check a country, open the HCCH status table and look for its name. As of 24 November 2026, Canada and China are listed, while Malaysia is not. The list keeps growing, so check again close to your application.

## How does the application work?

The HCCH names the Directorate General of Legal Administrative Affairs (Ditjen AHU) as Indonesia's competent authority. Under Regulation No. 6 of 2022 the steps are:

1. Apply electronically through the Ditjen AHU website. Fill in the form and upload your ID and the document. If someone applies for you, add a power of attorney and their ID.
2. Wait for verification, which the regulation caps at 3 working days from receipt.
3. Pay the official fee within 7 calendar days of the payment notice.
4. Collect the apostille certificate by showing the original document at the head office or a regional office counter.

Procedures and fees can be updated, so treat the official Ditjen AHU page as the final word.

## Where does translation fit in?

The regulation does not cover translation. The receiving institution decides whether it wants the original apostilled, a translation, or both, and in which order. Read its checklist carefully and ask by email when it is unclear, so you have the answer in writing. A common slip is translating first and learning later that the original needed an apostille, which adds a step and pushes the schedule back.

## FAQ

### Is an apostille the same as a sworn translation?
No. An apostille certifies the signature, stamp or seal on a document, while a translation carries its content into another language. An institution may ask for one or both.

### Do all countries accept an apostille?
No. It only works between countries that are party to the Apostille Convention. Check the HCCH status table for your destination.

### How long does verification take in Indonesia?
Regulation No. 6 of 2022 sets a maximum of 3 working days from receipt of the application. Payment and collection of the certificate follow after that.
