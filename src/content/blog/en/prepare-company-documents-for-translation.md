Translating a 30-page contract is not like translating a diploma. More is at stake, more people are involved, and one inconsistent term can start an argument. Here are the questions we hear most.

## "The document is still being revised. Can we send it anyway?"

Yes for review and a quote, but work should start from the final version. Every clause changed after translation means rework. If time is short, mark the parts that may still change.

## "Do annexes need translating?"

It depends on their role. A technical annex full of figures can sometimes be left as is, while an annex that sets out the parties' obligations almost always needs translating. Decide early so the quote is accurate.

## "We use our own terms. What then?"

That helps a great deal. Make a short list like this:

| Term used in the company | Usual equivalent |
|---|---|
| Perjanjian Kerja Sama | Cooperation Agreement |
| Direktur Utama | President Director |
| Surat Kuasa | Power of Attorney |

With that list, the translation will match the documents your company already has.

## "Sworn or regular?"

For internal reading or negotiation material, a regular translation is often enough. For filing with an authority, a court or a notary, a sworn translation is usually requested.

## "How much time should we allow?"

That depends on page count and complexity, so a figure can only be given once the document has been seen. What you control is when you send it. Put translation into the project schedule from the start, alongside legal review, not as the last step the night before signing.

## Checklist before sending

- [ ] The version is final and dated.
- [ ] All signed and stamped pages are included.
- [ ] You have decided which annexes are translated.
- [ ] A list of standard terms is attached.
- [ ] Party names are written as in their deeds.
- [ ] One person is named to answer the translator's questions.

## A familiar case

Two divisions of the same company send documents separately. One calls its head the *President Director*, the other uses *Chief Executive Officer*. When both documents reach the foreign partner, the partner asks whether these are two different people. The problem is not the translation. It is the lack of one shared term list.

## FAQ

### Are company documents kept confidential?
Documents are used only for the translation work. If your company needs special arrangements, raise them before sending anything.

### Can several documents be handled together?
Yes. Send the full set so the cost and time estimate covers everything.

### Who should review the result?
Someone who knows the original well, usually from the legal team or the people who drafted the contract.
