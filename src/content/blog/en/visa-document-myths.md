Visa matters come with plenty of stories from friends, group chats and forums. Some help, some mislead. These six come up most.

## Myth 1: "Every document must be translated"

**Fact:** only those requested in a particular language. Some missions accept documents in the original language for certain items, others do not.

## Myth 2: "Visa requirements are the same everywhere"

**Fact:** each country, and each visa type, has its own list. A student visa differs from a visitor visa.

## Myth 3: "A translation by a friend with good English is enough"

**Fact:** when an official translation is requested, what counts is not only whether the content is right but also who takes responsibility for it.

## Myth 4: "A bank statement is just numbers, no need to translate"

**Fact:** transaction descriptions, account types and column headings are text. The officer needs to understand all of it.

## Myth 5: "Once translated, nothing else is needed"

**Fact:** some destinations ask for legalization or an apostille as a separate step. Translation and authentication are two different things.

## Myth 6: "Sort the documents later, get the appointment first"

**Fact:** your interview slot will not wait for your file. Documents that are not ready on the day can mean starting over.

## Summary

| The belief | The reality |
|---|---|
| Everything must be translated | Only what is requested |
| Requirements are uniform | They vary by country and visa type |
| Numbers need no translation | The text around the numbers does |
| Translation equals authentication | Two separate processes |

## How to tell reliable information

1. It comes from the embassy's or visa centre's official page.
2. It shows a clear update date.
3. It names the visa type specifically.

Other people's experiences are useful for a general picture, but they do not replace the official list. Requirements change, and last year's experience may not apply today.

## What tends to happen

Someone copies a document list from a social media post by a person who travelled two years earlier. On submission day there turns out to be a new form and one additional document now required. The appointment has to be rebooked. Five minutes on the official page would have prevented it.

## FAQ

### Where are the most accurate visa requirements?
On the official website of the embassy or visa application centre of the destination country.

### Must visa documents be originals?
Rules differ. Some want originals shown, others accept copies. Follow the official instructions.

### How early should I start?
As early as possible once your travel plan is firm, because some stages run on timelines you cannot control.
