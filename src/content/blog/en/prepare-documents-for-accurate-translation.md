A translator works from what is visible. If the file is blurry, cropped or sent without context, the result is a string of questions or an "illegible" note in the translation. Most of that can be avoided on your side in under ten minutes.

## Why does preparation affect the translation?

Think of a tailor. Even a very good one needs the right measurements. With the wrong numbers, the suit is neatly made and still does not fit.

Document translation works the same way. The translator has to stay faithful to the source. They cannot guess a letter hidden under a stamp, fill in a page that was never sent, or pick one spelling of a name that exists in several versions. The clearer your material, the less room there is for error.

## 6 steps before you send a document

1. **Send every page.** That includes the back, attachments and pages that only carry a stamp or signature. They are part of the document.
2. **Make sure everything is legible.** Shoot from directly above, in good light, with all four corners showing. See our guide on [translating from scans or photos](/en/articles/can-scans-or-photos-be-translated/).
3. **Type out names as they appear in the passport.** Do not rely on the document alone. Add the names of parents or a spouse if they appear too.
4. **Explain the hard-to-read parts.** Handwriting, abbreviations of Indonesian offices, faded numbers. You often know what they say; the translator may not. They will still check your note against the document.
5. **Say what the translation is for.** University, visa, employment or court, and in which country. If the receiving institution has written requirements, send the link or a photo.
6. **Send related documents together.** A diploma with its transcript, or a birth certificate with the family card. Terms, names and date formats can then be kept consistent across the set.

## A sample message: before and after

Your cover message matters as much as the file.

| | Message that triggers questions | Message that is ready to work on |
|---|---|---|
| Request | "Please translate this." | "Please translate my Indonesian diploma and transcript into English for a master's application in the Netherlands." |
| Names | Not mentioned | "Name as in passport, typed out in full" |
| Attachment | One photo, front page only | All pages, front and back, in a single PDF |
| Notes | None | "The stamp at the bottom right covers the serial number. I have typed the full number below." |
| Requirements | None | Link to the university's requirements page |

The second message is longer, but it saves a lot of back-and-forth later.

## Parts of a document people forget to send

| Part | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Back page | Often carries endorsements, amendments or a certified-copy stamp |
| Margin notes on civil certificates | May record a change of name or status |
| Stamps and signatures | They are described in the translation |
| Letterhead and reference number | Used by the receiving institution to match documents |
| Grading scale on a transcript | Helps the university read your grades |

If you are unsure whether a page is needed, send it. Setting a page aside is easier than asking for a missing one.

## What if the original document contains an error?

This happens fairly often, for example a name on a birth certificate that differs from the passport by one letter. A translation follows the source, so it cannot "correct" the original. Mention the difference at the start so the translator knows it is not a typo. For the correction itself, ask the office that issued the document, and ask the receiving institution which supporting documents they accept.

## After you receive the translation

Good preparation lowers the risk, but still take a few minutes to read the result. Focus on names, dates and numbers. Our guide on [checking names, dates and numbers in a translation](/en/articles/check-names-dates-numbers-in-translation/) shows how.

## FAQ

### Do I need to send pages that only contain a stamp?
Yes. Pages with a stamp, signature or note are part of the document. Completely blank pages are not needed, but tell the translator how many pages the original has.

### Can I retype the document to make it clearer?
Not as a replacement. The translator needs to see the original as a photo or scan. Your typed text is useful as a helper note for parts that are hard to read.

### My name is spelled differently across documents. Which one is used?
The translation follows the spelling in the document being translated. Tell the translator your passport spelling and the difference from the start, then follow the receiving institution's official requirements on supporting documents.
